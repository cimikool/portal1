📄 Read the text

What Does It Mean to Be a Good Citizen?
Civic rights, responsibilities, and participation
Paragraph 1 — Introduction

Citizenship is not simply a legal status — it is an active commitment to the community and the country in which one lives. A good citizen understands that society functions well only when its members both exercise their rights and fulfil their responsibilities. Rights without responsibilities lead to disorder; responsibilities without rights lead to oppression.

Paragraph 2 — Rights

Democratic societies guarantee a set of fundamental rights to every citizen: the right to freedom of expression, the right to education, the right to vote, and the right to be treated with dignity and equality. These rights are usually protected by a constitution. They cannot be taken away without due legal process.

Paragraph 3 — Responsibilities

However, citizenship also demands responsibilities. Citizens are expected to obey the law, pay taxes, protect the environment, and participate in community life. Voting, for example, is simultaneously a right and a civic duty. A person who does not vote is failing to contribute to the democratic process.

Paragraph 4 — Active citizenship

The most valuable form of citizenship is active citizenship — going beyond the legal minimum to volunteer, campaign for change, and support those in need. Young people, in particular, have a vital role to play. They can join student councils, organise community projects, and use technology to raise awareness of social issues. As one youth leader said, "We should not wait to be given a voice — we ought to create one."

Reported speech in the text: Paragraph 4 contains a direct quotation — "We should not wait to be given a voice — we ought to create one." In an exam, you might be asked to report this: The youth leader said that they should not wait to be given a voice and that they ought to create one.
