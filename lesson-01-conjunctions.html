Conjunctions: and, but, so, because
A1.2 Common Core Moroccan Curriculum

🔗 What is a conjunction?

A conjunction is a word that connects two ideas in one sentence. Instead of writing two short sentences, we use a conjunction to join them.

In English at A1.2 level, we use four main conjunctions: and, but, so, and because.

📋 The four conjunctions

and
Adds information
I like tea and coffee.
but
Shows contrast
I like tea but not coffee.
so
Shows result
It was cold, so I wore a coat.
because
Gives reason
I wore a coat because it was cold.

💬 More examples

I have a brother and a sister. Adding two things together.
She speaks Arabic and French. Two abilities joined.
He is tall but his brother is short. Opposite ideas — surprising contrast.
I want to go out but it is raining. Wish vs. reality.
I was tired, so I went to bed early. Cause → Result.
The shop was closed, so we went home. Situation → Consequence.
She is happy because she passed her exam. Feeling ← Reason.
I study English because I love it. Action ← Motivation.

⚖️ so vs because

Students often confuse so and because. Remember this rule:

because introduces the REASON (why something happens)
so introduces the RESULT (what happens after)
I was hungry because I didn't eat breakfast. Why are you hungry? → Because I didn't eat.
I didn't eat breakfast, so I was hungry. Same meaning — different order and conjunction.
