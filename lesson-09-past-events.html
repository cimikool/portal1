Communication: Talking About Past Events
🎨 The Arts A1.2 Common Core

🎯 What you will learn

By the end of this lesson, you can ask and answer questions about past events in your own life — what you did, where you went, who you went with, and how you felt — using the simple past from Lesson 7.

In Lesson 5 you practised biography questions about famous artists. Now you move from artists to yourself. You will learn to talk about a film you saw, a concert you attended, or a museum you visited — the kind of conversation Moroccan students have in class and in exams.

❓ Questions about past events

What did you do last weekend?
I went to the cinema / a concert / a museum.
→ The most common opener — use it to start a conversation.
What did you see / watch?
I saw / watched an action film / a documentary / a concert.
→ Asks about the specific event or film.
Who did you go with?
I went with my family / friends / classmates.
→ Asks about the people you were with.
How did you like it? / What did you think of it?
I loved it / I enjoyed it / It was amazing / It was boring.
→ Asks for your opinion. Use adjectives from Lesson 8!
Where did you go?
I went to the cinema / the theatre / the national museum.
→ Asks about the place.
When did you go?
I went last Saturday / last week / yesterday.
→ Asks about the time. Use time expressions below!

🕐 Time expressions for the past

Always use these expressions with the simple past — never with the present tense.

yesterdaythe day before today
last weekendSaturday or Sunday
last weekthe week before
last nightthe evening before today
last monththe month before
last yearthe year before
two days ago48 hours before now
in 2023a specific past year
Position: Time expressions usually come at the start or end of the sentence — never between the subject and verb.
✓ Last weekend, I went to the cinema.
✓ I went to the cinema last weekend.
✗ I last weekend went to the cinema.

💬 Useful expressions — giving your opinion

Use these to sound natural when you talk about a film, concert, or exhibition.

I really enjoyed it. General positive opinion — safe and natural to use.
It was amazing / fantastic / brilliant. Strong positive — use for something you loved.
It was interesting / quite good. Mild positive — you liked it but it wasn't perfect.
I didn't enjoy it very much. / It was a bit boring. Negative — be polite when you didn't like something.
I found it very moving / inspiring / creative. Uses word formation vocabulary from Lesson 8! ✓
I would recommend it. To suggest that others should see/visit it too.

🎭 Model dialogue — at the museum

Karim and Nadia talk on Monday morning about their weekend.

Karim
What did you do last weekend, Nadia?
Nadia
I went to the Mohammed VI Museum of Modern Art with my parents.
Karim
Oh really! What did you see there?
Nadia
We saw an exhibition of Moroccan paintings and sculptures. It was incredible.
Karim
How did you like it?
Nadia
I loved it! I found it very inspiring. The paintings were so colourful and creative. I didn't want to leave!
Karim
Did you buy anything?
Nadia
No, I didn't. But I took a lot of photos! You should go — I would definitely recommend it.
Notice: Nadia uses vocabulary from across all Unit 7 lessons — paintings, sculptures, inspiring, creative — in a natural, everyday conversation. This is exactly what you should do!
