Vocabulary: Verb Endings & Word Formation
🎨 The Arts A1.2 Common Core

🎯 What you will learn

By the end of this lesson, you can pronounce the three endings of regular past verbs (/t/ /d/ /ɪd/), and build word families — noun, adjective, adverb — from arts vocabulary you already know.

This lesson has two parts. First, you will learn how to pronounce the -ed ending correctly — it is not always the same sound! Then you will learn how words change form depending on how you use them in a sentence.

🔊 Part 1 — How to pronounce -ed

The -ed ending of regular past verbs has three different sounds. The sound depends on the last sound of the base verb.

/t/
After voiceless sounds:
p, k, f, s, sh, ch
painted → /peɪnt/
worked → /wɜːkt/
stopped → /stɒpt/
/d/
After voiced sounds:
b, g, v, z, m, n, l, r + vowels
performed → /d/
created → /d/
moved → /muːvd/
/ɪd/
After /t/ or /d/ sounds only
started → /ɪd/
painted → /ɪd/
visited → /ɪd/
Simple trick: If the base verb ends in the letter t or d, the -ed ending adds a new syllable and sounds like /ɪd/ (you can hear it clearly). For all other verbs, it is just /t/ or /d/ — no extra syllable.

painted = 2 syllables → /ɪd/  ·  worked = 1 syllable → /t/  ·  performed = 2 syllables but no extra → /d/

🧩 Part 2 — Word formation

In English, one root word can become a noun, an adjective, or an adverb by adding a suffix. This helps you express ideas in different ways.

Common suffixes to know:

-ion / -tion → noun -ance / -ence → noun -ity → noun -ive → adjective -ful → adjective -al → adjective -ly → adverb
Verb Noun Adjective Adverb
create creation creative creatively
perform performance performing —
succeed success successful successfully
imagine imagination imaginative imaginatively
inspire inspiration inspiring —
express expression expressive expressively
compose composition — —
Focus on these four families — they appear most often in Unit 7 texts and exam tasks:
create → creation → creative → creatively
succeed → success → successful → successfully
perform → performance → performing
inspire → inspiration → inspiring

💬 Word families in context

Notice how the same root changes form depending on its role in the sentence.

Picasso was a very creative artist. (adjective — describes the artist) creative = adjective → describes a noun
His creation of Guernica in 1937 was a masterpiece. (noun) creation = noun → the thing he created
He painted creatively and used colours in a new way. (adverb) creatively = adverb → describes how he painted
Umm Kulthum's performance lasted for six hours. (noun) performance = noun → the event
She sang with great expression and emotion. (noun) expression = noun → comes from the verb "express"
Her voice was inspiring to millions of people. (adjective) inspiring = adjective → describes the voice
Chaplin was successful in both acting and directing. (adjective) successful = adjective → comes from the noun "success"
He successfully combined comedy and emotion in his films. (adverb) successfully = adverb → describes how he combined them
