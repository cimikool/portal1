Reading: A Mixed Marriage
🌍 Cultural Values 2nd Baccalaureate

🎯 Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you can read and understand a text about intercultural marriage, identify attitudes and values, and answer comprehension questions at exam level.

📖 Before you read — key terms

intercultural = between different cultures
customs = traditional practices of a culture
compromise = agree by each giving something up
prejudice = unfair negative opinion about a group

📄 Read the text

A Mixed Marriage
An account of life between two cultures
Paragraph 1

When Nadia, a young Moroccan woman, married David, a Canadian man, her family had mixed feelings. Her parents worried about the cultural differences, while her younger brother was excited about having a foreign brother-in-law. Nadia herself was optimistic. She believed that love and mutual respect could overcome any cultural barrier.

Paragraph 2

The first years of their marriage were not without difficulties. David found it hard to put up with certain customs that he did not understand at first — such as large extended family gatherings and different attitudes towards gender roles. For her part, Nadia had to adapt to a more individualistic lifestyle and a different way of celebrating occasions.

Paragraph 3

Over time, however, they learned to compromise. David picked up some Arabic words, dressed up for Moroccan celebrations, and even looked up recipes to cook traditional Moroccan dishes. Nadia, in turn, handed in a project at a local community centre about intercultural marriage, hoping to help others in similar situations.

Paragraph 4

Today, Nadia and David consider their mixed marriage a source of richness. Their children are being brought up bilingually and with an awareness of both cultures. "Our differences," Nadia says, "are not a problem — they are our greatest asset."

Phrasal verbs in the text: put up with, picked up, dressed up, looked up, handed in, brought up — all phrasal verbs from Lesson 4!

✏️ Quick practice

Choose the correct answer for each question.

1. Why did Nadia's parents have "mixed feelings" about the marriage?
They did not like David personally. They were worried about the cultural differences. They wanted Nadia to live in Canada. They did not approve of marriage.
2. What does "put up with" mean in Paragraph 2?
enjoy understand tolerate refuse
3. How did David show his effort to adapt to Moroccan culture?
He moved to Morocco permanently. He converted his religion. He learned Arabic words, dressed up for Moroccan celebrations, and cooked Moroccan dishes. He handed in a project about intercultural marriage.
4. What is Nadia and David's view of their differences today?
They are a source of conflict. They are a source of richness and their greatest asset. They are a problem to be solved. They are not important.
