Speaking: Rights and Responsibilities
🏛️ Citizenship 2nd Baccalaureate

🎯 Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you can discuss civic rights and responsibilities, give and justify opinions, and use reported speech to reference what others have said.

🗣️ Discussion questions — citizenship

What makes a good citizen?Think about: obeying laws, voting, volunteering, respecting others, protecting the environment.
What rights should young people have that they don't currently have?Think about: voting age, right to work, right to be heard in political decisions.
Is voting a right, a duty, or both? Should it be compulsory?Classic citizenship debate — use your Unit 7 vocabulary and advice language.
What responsibilities do students have at school and in society?Think about: respecting others, protecting school property, community participation.

🛠️ Useful language for civic discussions

Giving opinions: In my view… / As far as I'm concerned… / I strongly believe that…
Justifying: This is because… / The reason is that… / This shows that…
Contrasting: However… / On the other hand… / Despite this…
Using reported speech: As the text stated, citizens ought to… / The youth leader said that young people should…
Concluding: All things considered… / On balance… / Taking everything into account…

💬 Model discussion — is voting compulsory?

Fatima
In my view, voting should be compulsory because it is both a right and a responsibility. As the reading stated, a person who does not vote is failing to contribute to the democratic process.
Adam
I see your point, however I believe that forcing people to vote could lead to uninformed choices. On the other hand, if more people voted, politicians would be more accountable.
Fatima
I advise young people to get involved in politics even before they can vote — through student councils and community projects.
Adam
All things considered, I agree that active citizenship starts long before you cast your first ballot.
