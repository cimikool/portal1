Grammar: Simple Past of "to be"
🎨 The Arts A1.2 Common Core

🎯 What you will learn

By the end of this lesson, you can use was and were to talk about people and things in the past — especially to describe famous artists and their lives.

In Lesson 3 you learned the names of artists — painter, composer, actor. Now you will learn how to talk about them in the past. We use the past of "to be" to say who they were, where they were born, and what they were famous for.

📋 Was and Were — the basics

The verb "to be" has two past forms: was and were.

SubjectAffirmative (+)Negative (−)Short answer
Iwas an artistwas not / wasn'tYes, I was.
He / She / Itwas a painterwas not / wasn'tNo, he wasn't.
Youwere a singerwere not / weren'tYes, you were.
We / Theywere actorswere not / weren'tNo, they weren't.
Simple rule: Use was with I / he / she / it  •  Use were with you / we / they

⚙️ How to build sentences

✅ Positive
Subject + was / were + complement
❌ Negative
Subject + wasn't / weren't + complement
❓ Question
Was / Were + subject + complement ?

💬 Examples — artists in the past

Picasso was a Spanish painter. He / She / It → was ✓
Leonardo da Vinci was born in Italy in 1452. We use "was born" to talk about birth.
Umm Kulthum and Fairouz were famous Arab singers. Two people (they) → were ✓
Charlie Chaplin wasn't a painter. He was an actor. Negative + correction ✓
The first films weren't in colour. They were black and white. they → weren't ✓
Was Picasso Spanish? — Yes, he was. Question → short answer ✓
Were da Vinci and Picasso painters? — Yes, they were. Two people → were ✓

🖌️ A model biography — Pablo Picasso

Read this biography. Notice how was / were are used throughout.

Pablo Picasso
1881 – 1973 · Spanish painter & sculptor
Pablo Picasso was born in Málaga, Spain, in 1881.
His father was an art teacher.
Picasso was a very talented child. He was only 13 when he painted his first masterpiece.
His most famous paintings were Guernica and Les Demoiselles d'Avignon.
He was not only a painter — he was also a sculptor and a poet.
Picasso was one of the most influential artists of the 20th century. He was 91 years old when he died in 1973.
Notice: In biographies, we always use was / were because we are talking about someone who lived in the past. This is the most important grammar point of Unit 7!

💡 Common mistakes — be careful!

✗   He were a painter. WRONG — He / She / It always takes WAS, not were.
✓   He was a painter. CORRECT ✓
✗   They was famous actors. WRONG — They / We / You always take WERE, not was.
✓   They were famous actors. CORRECT ✓
Time expressions used with the simple past of be:
in 1881 · last year · yesterday · in the 20th century · long ago · at the age of 13
