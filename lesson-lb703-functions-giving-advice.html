Functions: Giving Advice
🏛️ Citizenship 2nd Baccalaureate

🎯 Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you can give advice using a range of expressions — from gentle suggestions to strong recommendations — in civic and everyday situations.

💡 Expressions for giving advice

ExpressionStrengthGrammar
You should…Neutral recommendationshould + base verb
You ought to…Moral obligation (slightly formal)ought to + base verb
You had better… (You'd better…)Strong — implies consequenceshad better + base verb
I advise you to…Formal recommendationadvise + object + to-inf
If I were you, I would…Personal / empatheticSecond conditional
If I were in your shoes, I would…Empathetic / idiomaticSecond conditional
Why don't you…?Gentle suggestionWhy don't + subject + base verb

💬 Examples — citizenship contexts

You should register to vote before the next election — it's your right and duty.Neutral advice → should + base verb ✓
You ought to respect the laws of your country even when you disagree with them.Moral obligation → ought to + base verb ✓
You'd better pay your taxes on time, or you may face legal consequences.Strong advice with implied consequence → had better ✓
If I were you, I would join a community volunteer group to become more involved.Personal advice → second conditional ✓
I advise you to report any discrimination you experience — you have the right to equality.Formal recommendation → advise + to-infinitive ✓
Why don't you start by attending a local council meeting to understand how decisions are made?Gentle suggestion → Why don't you + base verb ✓

⚠️ You'd better — important grammar note

"You had better" (you'd better) is NOT the same as "you should".
It implies a warning or consequence: if you don't follow the advice, something bad will happen.

✓ You'd better obey the law. (or you'll face consequences)
✓ You'd better register soon. (or the deadline will pass)

After "had better", always use the base verb (not to-infinitive):
✓ You'd better go.    ✗ You'd better to go.
